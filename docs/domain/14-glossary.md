# 14 — Glossary

A–Z of film industry terminology used across this library. Cross-references
point to the document where the term is explained in context.

> **Domain reference.** Real industry, not this game. See `README.md`.

---

## A

**ADR** — Automated Dialogue Replacement (or "looping"). Re-recording dialogue
in a studio to picture. `07`

**Above the Line (ATL)** — Story/rights, producers, director, principal cast.
`03` §9

**Additional photography** — Planned extra shooting days after the main
schedule, distinct from reshoots. `06` §10

**Answer print** — Historically, the first colour-corrected film print
approved for release; now largely superseded by the DI master. `07`

**Assembly** — The editor's first full cut, in script order. `07` §2

**Attachment** — A talent's commitment to a project. Soft = interested;
hard = signed. `03` §2

## B

**Backend** — Contingent compensation from a film's revenue. `11` §2

**Best boy** — Second in command of the grip or electric department. `05`

**Below the Line (BTL)** — Crew, equipment, and physical production costs.
`03` §9

**Blocking** — Staging actors and camera for a scene. `06` §3

**Breakdown** — Decomposition of a script into its physical elements. `04` §2

**Breakeven** — See "cash break-even", "net profit". `11` §6

**Buyout** — A lump sum replacing residuals and backend; streaming standard.
`11` §2

## C

**Certified Fresh** — Rotten Tomatoes' threshold badge, used in advertising. `16` §4

**Call sheet** — The next day's shooting instructions. `06` §2

**CARA** — The MPA's ratings board (G/PG/PG-13/R/NC-17). `01` §8.1

**Cash break-even** — The point at which the studio has recouped its actual
cash outlay; the usual modern participation trigger. `11` §2

**Chain of title** — Documented, unbroken ownership of the underlying rights.
`02` §3

**CinemaScore** — Opening-night audience letter grade; a strong predictor of
legs. `09` §7

**Circle take** — A take marked as preferred for the editor. `06` §3

**Company move** — Relocating the unit during a shooting day. `06` §5

**Completion bond** — A guarantee that the film will be completed and
delivered. `03` §7

**Contingency** — Budget reserve, typically 10%. `03` §10

**Conform** — Rebuilding the offline edit at full resolution for finishing.
`07` §6

**Cover set** — An interior kept ready in case exterior weather fails. `04` §3.1

**Coverage** — (1) The set of angles shot for a scene `06` §4; (2) A reader's
written assessment of a script `02` §5. Two unrelated meanings.

**Craft service** — Continuous on-set snacks and drinks, distinct from
catering. `05` §15

## D

**Definite recommend** — The exit-poll measure of whether a viewer will propagate the film. `16` §7

**Dailies / rushes** — The previous day's footage, viewed same-day. `06` §9

**Day-and-date** — Theatrical and home release simultaneously. `09` §7.2

**Day out of days (DOOD)** — The grid of which cast work which days. `04` §3.2

**Day player** — An actor engaged by the day for a small role. `04` §8

**DCP** — Digital Cinema Package, the deliverable cinemas play. `07` §6

**Deliverables** — The contractual schedule of items owed to the distributor.
`07` §7

**DI** — Digital Intermediate; conform, grade, and finishing. `07` §6

**DIT** — Digital Imaging Technician; on-set colour and data pipeline. `05` §5

**Distribution fee** — The studio's charge to itself against participations,
30–35% of rentals. `11` §2.1

**Drop/pickup** — Releasing and re-engaging an actor with a permitted gap.
`04` §3.2

## E

**E&O** — Errors and Omissions insurance. `03` §8

**EPK** — Electronic Press Kit; behind-the-scenes marketing material. `09` §8

**EST** — Electronic Sell-Through (digital purchase). `11` §3

**Eighths** — The unit of page measurement for scheduling. `04` §2

## F

**Favoured nations** — Everyone on identical terms. `07` §4.2, `12` §2

**Final cut** — The contractual right to determine the released version. `03` §3

**First-dollar gross** — Participation from the first dollar of receipts.
`11` §2

**Foley** — Performed sound effects (footsteps, cloth, props). `07` §3

**Four-quadrant** — A film appealing to all four demographic quadrants. `09` §5

**Four-wall** — Renting a screen outright and keeping the gross. `09` §7.2

**Fringes** — Payroll taxes and union benefits loaded onto labour, 20–35%.
`03` §9

**Front-loaded** — A film taking most of its gross early. `10` §3

## G

**Gaffer** — Chief lighting technician; heads the electrical department.
`05` §7

**Gap financing** — Lending against unsold territories. `03` §1

**Golden time** — A premium overtime tier. `06` §7

**Greenlight** — The decision to commit production funding. `02` §8

**Green band / red band** — All-audience vs restricted trailer. `09` §3

**Grip** — Camera support and light-shaping rigging. `05` §6

**Gross** — Total ticket sales, before the exhibitor's share. `10` §1

## H

**Heat** — Temporary market desirability following a breakout. `12` §4

**Hiatus** — A planned break in the shooting schedule. `06` §11

**Hold** — (1) Paid non-working day for an actor `04` §3.2;
(2) Reserved dates on a talent's calendar `03` §2.

**Holdover** — Retaining a film on screens for further weeks. `09` §7.3

## I

**Insert** — A close shot of an object or detail. `06` §4

## K

**Key art** — The poster and its derivative advertising imagery. `09` §3

**KDM** — Key Delivery Message; the decryption key for a DCP on a given
screen and date. `07` §6

## L

**Legs** — A film's ability to keep grossing after opening; the multiple.
`10` §4

**Line producer** — The producer responsible for the physical film's budget
and schedule. `05` §3

**Lock (picture lock)** — Editorial complete; downstream departments can
finish. `07` §1

**LOI** — Letter of intent; non-binding attachment. `03` §2

**LUT** — Look-Up Table; the on-set colour look applied to monitoring and
dailies. `05` §5

## M

**M&E** — Music and Effects mix, with no dialogue; required for foreign
dubbing. `07` §3

**Martini** — The last shot of the shooting day. `06` §3

**Master** — The wide shot covering an entire scene. `06` §4

**Master use licence** — Permission to use a specific recording. `07` §4.2

**Meal penalty** — Escalating payments for breaking a meal late. `06` §7

**MG (minimum guarantee)** — A distributor's guaranteed payment for territory
rights. `03` §4

**Multiple** — Total gross ÷ opening weekend. `10` §4

## N

**Negative cost** — The all-in cost of producing the finished film. `11` §5

**Negative pickup** — A pre-agreed purchase of the finished film on delivery.
`03` §5

**Net profit** — Revenue after every contractual deduction; famously rarely
positive. `11` §2.1

## O

**Offer-only** — Talent who do not audition; cast through lists, meetings, and offers. `04` §8.1

**Oner** — A scene covered in one continuous take. `06` §4

**One-liner** — The condensed shooting schedule. `04` §3.3

**Option** — The time-limited exclusive right to buy underlying material.
`02` §3

**OTS** — Over-the-shoulder shot. `06` §4

**Output deal** — A territory distributor commits to a studio's whole slate.
`01` §9

**Overage** — Spending above budget, or working beyond contracted days.
`03` §10, `12` §6

**Overhead charge** — The studio's fee to itself, 10–15% of negative cost.
`11` §2.1

## P

**Photo scout** — Photographic survey of candidate locations. `04` §5.1

**P&A** — Prints and Advertising; the marketing budget. `09` §1

**Package** — Director, cast, and writer assembled as a unit. `01` §5.1, `03` §2

**Pay-or-play** — Owed the fee whether or not the film is made. `03` §2.1

**Per-screen average (PSA)** — Gross ÷ locations. `10` §1

**Pickups** — Small missing shots gathered after the main shoot. `06` §10

**Platform release** — Opening small and expanding on strength. `09` §7.2

**Postvis** — Temporary VFX cut into the edit for evaluation. `08` §1.2

**Previs** — Animated pre-planning of sequences. `08` §1.2

**Production report (DPR)** — The daily record of what was shot and what it
cost. `06` §2

**PVOD** — Premium Video On Demand; early, higher-priced home rental. `11` §3

## Q

**Quadrant** — Demographic segmentation (male/female, over/under 25). `09` §5

**Quote** — A talent's most recent fee, which sets their next floor. `12` §2

## R

**Rentals** — The distributor's share of box office gross. `10` §1

**Reshoots** — Re-shooting material that didn't work. `06` §10

**Residuals** — Guild-mandated reuse payments, independent of profit. `11` §4

**Reversion** — Rights returning to the author when an option lapses. `02` §3

**Rough cut** — The cut between assembly and fine cut. `07` §2

## S

**Sides** — The scene pages an actor is given to audition with. `04` §8.1

**Scale** — A guild's minimum rate. `12` §7

**Script supervisor** — Guardian of continuity and the editorial record.
`05` §3

**Second unit** — A parallel crew shooting material without the principals.
`05` §16

**Setup** — A new camera position; the unit in which a day's work is counted.
`04` §3.4

**SFX** — Practical, on-set special effects (distinct from VFX). `08` §2

**Slate** — (1) A studio's portfolio of films `11` §5;
(2) The clapperboard `05` §5.

**Spec script** — A screenplay written without a buyer. `02` §2.1

**Squib** — A practical bullet-hit effect. `08` §2

**Stripboard** — The scheduling board of scene strips. `04` §3.1

**Sync licence** — Permission to use a composition against picture. `07` §4.2

## T

**Tomatometer** — Percentage of reviews that are positive; a consensus measure, not a quality one. `16` §4

**Trailer house** — Outside specialist that cuts trailers, often competitively. `09` §2

**Techvis** — Previs converted into camera and rig specifications. `08` §1.2

**Temp love** — Attachment to the temporary score. `07` §4.1

**Tentpole** — A large film a studio's year is built around. `01`

**Test screening** — A recruited audience preview with scored cards. `07` §9

**Tracking** — Pre-release survey data on awareness and interest. `09` §7

**Turnaround** — (1) Minimum rest between wrap and next call `06` §7;
(2) A studio releasing a project for another buyer to take `02` §9;
(3) Repositioning the camera for the reverse angle `06` §3. Three unrelated
meanings — context distinguishes them.

**Turnover** — Delivering locked shots to VFX vendors. `08` §1.2

**TVOD** — Transactional Video On Demand; per-title rental or purchase. `11` §3

## U

**Under-five** — A performer with fewer than five lines; a specific SAG
category. `04` §8

**UPM** — Unit Production Manager. `05` §3

## V

**VFX** — Visual effects created in post. `08` §1

**Video village** — The monitors where director and producers watch. `05` §5

**Virtual production** — LED volumes and real-time in-camera backgrounds.
`08` §1.4

## W

**Wild wall** — A removable set wall, allowing camera positions a real room won't. `04` §5.5

**Walla** — Crowd background voices, recorded as group ADR. `07` §3

**Window** — The exclusive period for one distribution channel. `09` §7.4

**Wrap** — The end of a shooting day, or of the shoot. `06` §12
