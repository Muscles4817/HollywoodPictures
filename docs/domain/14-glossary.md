# 14 — Glossary

A–Z of film industry terminology used across this library. Cross-references
point to the document where the term is explained in context.

> **Domain reference.** Real industry, not this game. See `README.md`.

---

## A

**Abandonment** — Permanently stopping a production, as opposed to suspending
it; costs are written off. `03` §14.1

**Above the Line (ATL)** — Story and rights, producers, director, and
principal cast — the budget's negotiated half. `03` §9

**Academy (AMPAS)** — The Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences, whose
branch-based membership votes the Oscars. `13` §2

**Account structure** — The numbered chart of accounts a budget is organised
into, shared across the industry. `04` §4.1

**Acquisition** — Buying a finished or near-finished film, usually at a
festival or from a screening. `03` §5.2

**Actuals** — Money actually spent and invoiced to date, as distinct from
committed or estimated cost. `03` §10.1

**Additional photography** — Planned extra shooting days after the main
schedule, distinct from reshoots. `06` §10

**Adjusted gross** — A participation definition sitting between first-dollar
gross and net: gross less specified deductions. `11` §2

**ADR** — Automated Dialogue Replacement ("looping"). Re-recording dialogue to
picture in a studio. `07` §3.3

**Adverse selection** — The risk that a co-financier is offered the films the
studio least wants to keep. `11` §7.3

**Aerial unit** — A parallel unit shooting from helicopters, cranes, or
drones, with its own safety regime. `06` §11.1

**AFM** — The American Film Market, one of the annual territory-sales markets.
`01` §9.4

**Ager / dyer** — Costume specialist who distresses and weathers garments to
match the story. `05` §9

**Aggregate deal** — Exhibition terms expressed as a single percentage of
gross across the run, rather than a sliding scale. `10` §1.2

**Aggregator** — Rotten Tomatoes or Metacritic; converts individual reviews
into a single published number. `16` §4.1

**Aggregator-approved critic** — A reviewer whose work counts toward the
aggregate score; approval is granted and can be revoked. `16` §2.4

**Allocation** — The stage of booking at which a circuit commits specific
locations and screen counts. `01` §8.2

**Amortisation** — Writing off a film's negative cost against revenue in
proportion to the revenue actually earned. `11` §5.3

**Animatronics** — Mechanised creature or character puppetry, radio- or
cable-controlled. `08` §8.1

**Answer print** — Historically the first colour-corrected print approved for
release; now a delivery formality superseded by the DI master. `02` §7.5

**Appliance** — A prosthetic piece glued to a performer; the unit prosthetic
makeup is built and costed in. `08` §4.2

**Arbitration (credit)** — The WGA's blind process for deciding writing credit
among multiple writers. `02` §7

**Armourer** — The specialist responsible for weapons on set, their handling,
and their safety regime. `08` §2.1

**Art director** — The production designer's deputy, translating design into
buildable drawings and running the drawing office. `05` §8.2

**Assembly** — The editor's first full cut, in script order, ready shortly
after wrap. `07` §2.1

**Assistant editor** — The editorial crew member who manages media, sync, temp
elements, and the flow of material to and from vendors. `05` §14

**Atmospherics** — Practical smoke, haze, rain, snow, and wind produced by the
SFX department. `08` §2.2

**Attachment** — A talent's commitment to a project; soft means interested,
hard means contractually signed. `03` §2

**Attrition funnel** — The steep narrowing from projects in development to
films actually made. `02` §1.2

**Audience score** — An aggregator's user-voted number, distinct from the
critic score and vulnerable to manipulation. `16` §4.3

**Audit (participation)** — A participant's contractual right to examine the
studio's accounting of their film. `11` §2.4

**Avail check** — An enquiry to an agent about whether a client is free on
given dates, without making an offer. `12` §2.4

**AVOD / FAST** — Ad-supported on-demand and free ad-supported streaming
television; the tail end of the revenue windows. `11` §3.1

**Awards consultant** — The outside strategist hired to run a film's or a
performer's awards campaign. `13` §5.1

## B

**Backend** — Contingent compensation paid out of a film's revenue rather than
as a fee. `11` §2

**Backlot** — A studio's permanent exterior street sets, controllable in a way
a real location is not. `04` §14.2

**Bake-off** — Competitive pitching by several writers for the same open
writing assignment. `02` §4.1

**Bankability** — The extent to which a name reliably changes what a film can
raise or open. `12` §3

**Beat sheet** — The scene-by-scene outline of an action sequence before it is
shot. `08` §3.4

**Below the Line (BTL)** — Crew, equipment, and physical production costs —
the half of the budget that is scheduled rather than negotiated. `03` §9

**Best boy** — Second in command of the grip or the electrical department.
`05` §7

**Bid package** — The material sent to VFX vendors so they can price the work:
cut, breakdown, plates, and methodology notes. `08` §1.7

**Billing block** — The credit block at the bottom of a poster, whose order
and size are contractually fixed. `05` §18.2

**Block (schedule)** — A contiguous group of shooting days sharing a location,
unit, or cast configuration. `04` §15.1

**Blocking** — Staging actors and camera for a scene, done on the floor before
lighting begins. `06` §3.2

**Bond** — See completion bond. `03` §7

**Booking** — The negotiation and commitment of a film onto an exhibitor's
screens. `09` §7.6

**Boom operator** — The sound department member who places and moves the
microphone through the take. `05` §11.1

**Box office bonus** — A cash payment triggered by defined box-office
thresholds, common in place of gross participation. `03` §3

**Box rental / kit fee** — A weekly payment for a crew member's own equipment,
separate from their labour rate. `05` §17.1

**Branch** — The Academy's craft divisions; each nominates in its own
category. `13` §2.1

**Break figure / break** — The gross above which the exhibitor's percentage
share increases under a sliding scale. `10` §1.2

**Breakdown (script)** — The decomposition of a script into every physical
element each scene requires. `04` §2

**Breakdown sheet** — The per-scene record produced by the breakdown, listing
cast, props, effects, and every other element. `04` §2.1

**Break-even** — The point at which a film has recovered its costs; the
definition varies by whose costs are counted. `11` §6

**Breakout** — The role or film that converts an unknown into a known
quantity. `12` §4.1

**Burn** — The daily cost of running a production; the number that makes lost
time expensive. `06` §1

**Business affairs** — The studio department that negotiates and papers deals,
distinct from the creative executives. `03` §3.3

**Business manager** — The person who handles a client's money — taxes,
investments, and payments — distinct from the agent and the manager. `12` §1.1

**Buyer (art dept.)** — The set-decoration crew member who sources and
purchases dressing. `05` §9

**Buyer (film)** — See film buyer. `01` §8.3

**Buyout** — A lump sum replacing residuals and backend; the streaming
standard. `11` §2

## C

**Calendar war** — The manoeuvring by which distributors stake, defend, and
vacate release dates against each other. `09` §7.5

**Call sheet** — The document issued the night before, telling each person
where to be, when, and for what. `06` §2.2

**Call to first shot** — The elapsed time between crew call and the first
take; the standard diagnostic of a well-run day. `06` §3.4

**CAM (collection account manager)** — The neutral third party that receives
revenues on an independent film and pays them out per the waterfall. `03` §1.1

**Capital stack** — The layered set of money sources funding a film, each with
its own priority and cost. `03` §1

**CARA** — The MPA's ratings administration board (G/PG/PG-13/R/NC-17).
`01` §8.1

**Card** — A single screen of main titles; position and shared-card status are
negotiated credit terms. `05` §18.1

**Cash break-even** — The point at which the studio has recouped its actual
cash outlay; the usual modern participation trigger. `11` §2

**Cast insurance** — Cover against a principal's illness, injury, or death
delaying or stopping the production. `03` §8

**Casting director** — The person who finds, reads, and negotiates the actors
for every role below the offer-only tier. `04` §8

**Catalogue sale** — An outright sale of a library to a new owner. `11` §12.3

**Category placement** — Deciding which awards category a performance or film
is campaigned in — lead versus supporting, for example. `13` §5.10

**Catering** — The hot-meal service for cast and crew, distinct from craft
service. `05` §15

**Certificate of authorship** — The signed instrument by which a writer's work
is assigned into the chain of title. `02` §3.3

**Certificate of engagement** — The short document confirming a loan-out
company's provision of an individual's services. `12` §6.1

**Certification** — A territory's ratings or censorship approval, which can
require cuts. `09` §15.2

**Certified Fresh** — Rotten Tomatoes' threshold badge, used in advertising.
`16` §4.2

**Chain of title** — Documented, unbroken ownership of the underlying rights.
`02` §3

**Change order** — A formal, priced amendment to an awarded VFX bid; the
mechanism by which scope growth is meant to be paid for. `08` §1.7

**Chemistry read** — An audition pairing shortlisted actors to test how they
play together. `04` §8.1

**China quota** — The limited number of foreign films admitted on
revenue-share terms each year. `01` §9.6

**CinemaScore** — Opening-night audience letter grade; a strong predictor of
legs. `16` §7

**Circle take** — A take marked by the director as preferred, flagged for the
editor. `06` §9.3

**Circuit** — An exhibition chain, booking as a single counterparty across
many locations. `01` §8.2

**Clearance (rights)** — Confirming that every element in a film — music,
marks, likenesses, artwork — can lawfully be used. `02` §3.2

**Clearance (zone)** — An exhibitor's contractual exclusivity over a film
within a geographic zone. `10` §1.2

**Collection account** — The neutral account through which an independent
film's receipts flow before distribution. `03` §1.1

**Colour system (revisions)** — The fixed sequence of paper colours
identifying successive script revisions. `04` §11.3

**Colourist** — The DI artist who sets the film's final image, shot by shot,
with the DP and director. `07` §6.2

**Comedy punch-up** — A short writing pass adding jokes without restructuring.
`02` §4.4

**Commission** — The agent's percentage of a client's earnings, conventionally
10%. `01` §5.7

**Company move** — Relocating the unit during a shooting day; one of the
largest avoidable time sinks. `06` §3.4

**Completion bond** — A guarantee to financiers that the film will be finished
and delivered, backed by a right to take over. `03` §7

**Completion guarantor** — The company issuing the bond, which monitors cost
and schedule throughout. `03` §7

**Composer** — The writer of the original score, engaged late and working to
the locked picture. `07` §4.1

**Comps** — Comparable prior films used to estimate what a project will earn.
`02` §8.2

**Concept art** — Illustration that establishes the look of a world before
anything is built. `04` §6.2

**Conform** — Rebuilding the offline edit at full resolution for finishing.
`07` §6.1

**Consensus (critical)** — The settled aggregate view of a film, which can
move substantially after release. `16` §11.2

**Construction coordinator** — The head of the build crew, responsible for
turning drawings into standing sets on schedule. `05` §8.5

**Contingency** — Budget reserve against the unforeseen, typically around 10%.
`03` §10

**Continuity** — Consistency of action, wardrobe, props, and eyeline across
takes and setups; owned by the script supervisor. `05` §3.4

**Continuity stills** — Photographs recording costume, hair, and makeup state
so they can be matched later. `04` §7.4

**Corridor** — A stretch of the release calendar with a particular competitive
and audience character. `09` §7.1

**Cost report** — The weekly document comparing budget, actuals, commitments,
and forecast final cost. `03` §10

**Cost-plus** — The streaming commissioning model: budget plus a fixed margin,
with no backend. `01` §7

**Costume plot** — The grid of which costume each character wears in each
scene. `04` §7.1

**Costume supervisor** — The costume department's logistics and continuity
head, running the truck and the crew. `05` §9

**Cover set** — An interior kept ready in case exterior weather fails.
`06` §5.3

**Coverage** — (1) The set of angles shot for a scene `06` §4; (2) A reader's
written assessment of a submitted script `02` §5. Two unrelated meanings.

**Craft legibility** — How visible a film's technical achievement is to
voters, which drives craft nominations. `13` §9.3

**Craft service** — Continuous on-set snacks and drinks, distinct from
catering. `05` §15

**Crew call** — The time the general crew is due on set, as distinct from the
shooting call. `06` §2.2

**Crisis management** — The publicity discipline of containing a scandal or a
leak before release. `09` §9.5

**Cross-collateralisation** — Netting one film's or territory's losses against
another's profits before a participation is calculated. `11` §2.2

**Cross-shooting** — Running two cameras on opposing angles simultaneously.
`06` §4.2

**Crowd simulation** — Digitally generated crowds driven by rules rather than
animated individually. `08` §1.3

**Cue sheet** — The itemised list of every music cue and its rights, required
for delivery and royalty payment. `07` §4.2

**Cult** — A film whose standing is built after release by a committed
audience rather than at release. `16` §11.3

**Cultural test** — The points-based qualification a film must pass for some
national incentives. `03` §6.1

**Cume** — Cumulative gross to date. `10` §1.1

**Cutaway** — A shot away from the main action, used for rhythm and for repair
in the edit. `06` §4

## D

**Dailies / rushes** — The previous day's footage, processed overnight and
reviewed same-day. `06` §9

**Data wrangler** — The camera-department role that offloads, verifies, and
backs up the day's media. `05` §5

**Day for night** — Shooting in daylight and grading to read as night.
`06` §6.2

**Day out of days (DOOD)** — The grid of which cast work, hold, or travel on
which days. `04` §11.4

**Day player** — An actor engaged by the day for a small role. `04` §3.1

**Day rate** — The standard unit of crew pay, before overtime, kit, and
fringes. `05` §17.2

**Day-and-date** — Releasing simultaneously in two channels, or across
territories, rather than sequentially. `09` §7.2

**DCP** — Digital Cinema Package, the encrypted deliverable cinemas play.
`07` §6.1

**Deal memo** — The short binding document setting the deal's key terms,
signed long before the long form. `12` §6.1

**Deferment** — Compensation postponed to a defined later payment point rather
than reduced. `12` §2.3

**Definite interest** — The tracking measure of how many aware respondents say
they intend to see a film. `09` §8.1

**Definite recommend** — The exit-poll measure of whether a viewer will
propagate the film; the best single predictor of legs. `16` §7.3

**Deliverables** — The contractual schedule of picture, sound, legal, and
marketing items owed to the distributor. `07` §14

**DGA** — The Directors Guild of America, whose agreement sets the director's
cut and other protections. `12` §7.1

**DI (digital intermediate)** — Conform, grade, and picture finishing. `07` §6

**Dialogue editing** — The sound-post pass that cleans, conforms, and smooths
recorded dialogue. `07` §3.3

**Diligence** — A financier's investigation of a project's rights, budget,
elements, and people before committing. `01` §4.3

**Direct distribution fee** — See distribution fee. `11` §2.1

**Director's cut** — (1) The contractually guaranteed cut a DGA director
delivers before the studio's `07` §2.1; (2) An alternative released version.
`12` §12.7

**Director's letter** — The document in which a director sets out their
intended version of the film, used to secure finance and cast. `03` §2.3

**Distant location** — A location far enough from the studio zone that
housing, per diem, and travel apply. `06` §7.1

**Distribution chief** — The executive who dates films, sets patterns, and
negotiates with circuits. `10` §12.3

**Distribution fee** — The studio's charge to itself against participations,
conventionally 30–35% of rentals. `11` §2.1

**DIT** — Digital Imaging Technician; on-set colour and data pipeline. `05` §5

**Dolly grip** — The grip who operates the dolly and executes camera movement
with the operator. `05` §6.1

**Domestic** — US and Canada combined, the standard reporting unit. `10` §1.1

**DPR (daily production report)** — The daily record of what was shot, what
was lost, and what it cost. `06` §2.3

**Draft** — A delivered version of the screenplay, and the unit a step deal is
paid in. `02` §4.3

**Draughtsperson** — The art-department drafter producing construction
drawings from the designer's intent. `05` §8

**Drop** — The percentage fall in weekend gross from one weekend to the next;
the second-weekend drop is the key quality signal. `10` §3

**Drop/pickup** — Releasing and re-engaging an actor across a permitted gap
rather than paying through it. `04` §3.2

**Dubbing** — Replacing the dialogue track in another language, standard in
some territories and resisted in others. `10` §9.3

## E

**Eighths** — The unit of page measurement — one eighth of a script page —
used for scheduling. `04` §2

**Elevator / logline** — The one-sentence version of a film everyone attached
must be able to give. `02` §13.1

**Eligibility** — The Academy's rules on what qualifies a film to be
considered in a given year. `13` §4

**Embargo** — The agreed time before which a review or reaction may not be
published. `16` §3.1

**End crawl** — The scrolling end credits, whose order is largely conventional
and partly negotiated. `05` §18.2

**E&O** — Errors and Omissions insurance, covering rights and clearance
claims; a delivery requirement. `03` §8.1

**EPK** — Electronic Press Kit; behind-the-scenes material cut for press and
marketing use. `09` §3.1

**Equity** — The riskiest money in the capital stack: last to recoup, first to
lose. `03` §1.5

**Equity bridge** — Short-term borrowing against a committed equity investment
not yet drawn. `11` §8.2

**Escalator** — A contractual increase in fee or participation triggered by a
defined event. `12` §2.3

**Essential elements** — The named director and cast whose replacement voids a
pre-sale or finance commitment. `03` §4.4

**EST** — Electronic Sell-Through; digital purchase. `11` §3.1

**Establishing shot** — A wide shot placing a scene in its location. `06` §4

**Estimate to complete (ETC)** — The forecast cost of the work remaining,
added to actuals to give the estimated final cost. `03` §10.1

**Estimated final cost (EFC)** — The current forecast of what the film will
finally cost. `03` §10.1

**Event-ness** — A film's quality of being something to attend rather than
merely watch; a driver of opening and repeat. `10` §16.3

**Executive producer** — A credit covering financiers, packagers, and
rights-holders; its meaning varies widely. `05` §3

**Exhibitor** — The cinema owner or circuit that plays the film and takes the
first share of the gross. `01` §8.2

**Exit polling** — Surveying an audience as it leaves the opening screenings;
CinemaScore and PostTrak are the instruments. `16` §7

## F

**Fandom** — A pre-existing committed audience for the underlying property,
which predicts opening far better than it predicts legs. `16` §12.1

**FAST** — Free Ad-Supported Streaming Television; a late, low-rate window.
`11` §3.1

**Favoured nations** — A term guaranteeing that no comparable party gets
better terms. `12` §2.3

**Fee drag** — The accumulation of layered fees that erodes a co-financier's
return. `11` §7.3

**Festival premiere** — A film's first public screening at a festival, used to
launch reviews, sales, or an awards campaign. `16` §3

**Field marketing** — Regional promotion, screenings, and partnerships run
city by city. `09` §2

**Fight choreographer** — The stunt-department specialist who designs and
rehearses hand-to-hand and weapon action. `08` §3.1

**Film buyer** — The exhibitor-side executive who decides which films a
circuit books, on what terms, and for how long. `01` §8.3

**Film rental** — The distributor's share of box-office gross, after the
exhibitor's. `10` §1.2

**Final cut** — The contractual right to determine the released version.
`12` §12.7

**Fine cut** — The cut after the rough cut, structurally settled and close to
lock. `07` §2.1

**First look image** — The controlled first still released from a production,
a staged step in the pre-release sentiment timeline. `16` §12.2

**First position / second position** — The ranking of competing commitments on
a talent's calendar; first position wins the dates. `12` §5.6

**First refusal** — A right to match or decline before someone else may take a
crew member or a project. `04` §9.5

**First team** — The principal actors, as distinct from the stand-ins ("second
team"). `12` §11.2

**First-dollar gross** — Participation from the first dollar of receipts, with
almost no deductions; now rare. `11` §2

**First-look deal** — An arrangement giving a studio first refusal on a
producer's projects in exchange for overhead. `01` §3

**Fittings** — Costume sessions with the actor, the department's main
lead-time constraint. `04` §7.1

**Focus group** — A small moderated discussion held after a test screening.
`07` §9.1

**Focus puller (1st AC)** — The camera crew member responsible for keeping the
image sharp through the take. `05` §5

**Foley** — Performed sound effects — footsteps, cloth, props — recorded to
picture. `07` §3.1

**Force majeure** — The contractual clause suspending obligations for events
outside anyone's control. `03` §14.2

**Forced call** — Calling a performer or crew member back before their
turnaround has elapsed, at penalty. `06` §7.1

**Four-quadrant** — A film appealing across both gender and the over/under-25
age split. `09` §6

**Four-wall** — Renting a screen outright and keeping the gross. `09` §7.2

**Franchise** — A property expected to sustain repeated films; its own
audience and its own fatigue. `09` §6.2

**Free weeks** — Contractually unpaid additional services — reshoots, ADR,
press — whose number is a negotiated point. `03` §3.6

**Frequency** — How often the target audience sees the campaign; traded
against reach in the media plan. `09` §5.2

**Fresh / Rotten** — Rotten Tomatoes' binary classification of an individual
review, and of the aggregate. `16` §4.2

**Fringes** — Payroll taxes and union benefits loaded onto labour, typically
20–35%. `03` §9

**Front-loaded** — A film taking most of its gross in the opening days; the
signature of demand without advocacy. `10` §1.1

**Frontrunner** — The film or performance perceived to be leading an awards
race; a position that attracts attack. `13` §5.7

**FYC** — "For Your Consideration": awards-season advertising and screening
activity aimed at voters. `13` §5.9

## G

**Gaffer** — Chief lighting technician; heads the electrical department.
`05` §7.1

**Gap loan** — Lending against the value of unsold territories. `03` §1.5

**General meeting** — A no-agenda meeting between a writer or director and an
executive, used to build relationships. `12` §1.4

**Golden time** — A punitive overtime tier reached deep into a long day.
`06` §7.1

**Grade** — The colour pass that establishes the finished image, done in the
DI. `07` §6.1

**Green band / red band** — All-audience versus restricted trailer. `09` §4

**Greenlight** — The decision to commit production funding. `02` §8

**Greensman** — The art-department specialist handling plants and landscape
dressing. `05` §8

**Grip** — Camera support and light-shaping rigging; the department that moves
and mounts everything the camera and lamps sit on. `05` §6

**Gross** — Total ticket sales, before the exhibitor's share. `10` §1.1

**Gross receipts** — The defined revenue pool from which a participation is
computed; its definition is the whole negotiation. `11` §2.3

**GRPs / TRPs** — Gross (or Target) Rating Points: reach × frequency, the
currency of a broadcast media plan. `09` §5.2

## H

**Hair department head** — The head of hair, responsible for design,
continuity, and the shooting-day schedule. `05` §10

**Hair punching** — Inserting hair strand by strand into a prosthetic
appliance; slow, and the difference between convincing and not. `08` §4.2

**Hard lock** — A release date the studio commits to and will defend rather
than move. `09` §7.1

**Head of department (HOD)** — The lead of a craft department, hired by the
director and producer and hiring their own crew. `04` §9

**Head of marketing** — The executive who owns positioning, creative, and the
P&A number. `09` §2.3

**Heat** — Temporary market desirability following a breakout, which decays if
not spent. `12` §4.2

**Heavy-up** — A late increase in media spend, usually in the final week
before release. `09` §5.4

**Hero prop** — A prop built to close-up standard, as distinct from stunt or
background duplicates. `04` §2.6

**Hiatus** — A planned break in the shooting schedule. `06` §11.2

**Hold** — (1) A paid non-working day for a contracted actor `04` §3.2; (2)
Reserved dates on a talent's calendar, ranked by position `12` §5.6.

**Holdover** — Retaining a film on screens for further weeks; sometimes a
contractual requirement of the booking. `09` §7.7

**Hook** — The single idea a campaign sells; finding it is the core
positioning problem. `09` §6.2

**Hot set** — A dressed set that must not be disturbed because shooting will
resume on it. `06` §8.1

**House nut / house allowance** — The exhibitor's agreed weekly operating
cost, deducted before the split under a 90/10 deal. `10` §1.2

## I

**IATSE** — The below-the-line crafts union covering most US crew departments.
`01` §6.1

**Incentive** — A territory's tax credit, rebate, or subsidy that reduces net
production cost. `03` §6

**Index** — A performance measure expressed against a norm or a comparable
film. `10` §1.1

**Individual-film-forecast method** — The accounting rule requiring negative
cost to be amortised in proportion to forecast total revenue. `11` §5.3

**Initiation fee** — The one-off cost of joining a guild, on top of ongoing
dues. `12` §7.4

**Insert** — A close shot of an object or detail. `06` §4

**Insurance** — The production package covering cast, equipment, liability,
and errors and omissions. `03` §8

**Interim finance** — Short-term borrowing that bridges the gap between a
committed source and its actual payment. `11` §8.2

**Internal multiplier** — Total gross divided by opening weekend, computed
inside the run rather than at the end. `10` §1.1

**International sales** — Licensing an independent film territory by territory
rather than distributing it directly. `12` §3.1

**Interparty agreement** — The document by which every financier on an
independent film agrees the order of recoupment. `03` §1.3

**Wide release** — Opening on several thousand screens at once, the default
